List 1 · Lecture 1 exercises

Attempt each problem on paper before revealing the solution. The summaries below preserve the relevant conditions; the original task list is the reference for the full wording.

The PDF skips Exercise 5 and labels two different tasks “Exercise 6.” They are distinguished below by topic. Exercise 7’s wording conflicts with its apparent intended comparison; both readings are explained.
Exercise 1 · Three components: sample space and events

Hint: preserve the order of the three components. “At least one fails” is easiest as “everything except WWW.”

\(\Omega=\{WWW,WWF,WFW,WFF,FWW,FWF,FFW,FFF\}\).

\(A=\{WWF,WFW,WFF,FWW,FWF,FFW,FFF\}\); \(B=\{WWF,WFW,FWW\}\); \(C=\{WWF,WFF\}\).

Since \(B\subseteq A\), \(A\cap B=B\) and \(A\cup B=A\). Finally, \(A^c=\{WWW\}\).

Exercise 2 · Four transmitted bits

Hint: C and E describe reception status, not the original bit values.

\(\Omega=\{CCCC,CCCE,CCEC,CCEE,CECC,CECE,CEEC,CEEE,ECCC,ECCE,ECEC,ECEE,EECC,EECE,EEEC,EEEE\}\). There are \(2^4=16\) outcomes.

\(A=\Omega\setminus\{CCCC\}\) (all 15 strings with at least one E). \(B=\{CCCC,CCEC,CECC,CEEC\}\) fixes the first and last positions as C. \(A^c=\{CCCC\}\): all four bits are received correctly.

An event neither contained in A nor contained in Aᶜ is \(D=\{CCCC,ECCC\}\). It includes one outcome from each.

Exercise 3 · Sensor resolution and interval endpoints

Whole degrees: \(\{0,1,\ldots,100\}\), 101 outcomes. Tenths: \(\{0,0.1,0.2,\ldots,100\}\), 1,001 outcomes. Any real value: \([0,100]\).

For \(A=[20,50]\), \(A^c=[0,20)\cup(50,100]\): below 20 or above 50, still within 0 to 100. Neither 20 nor 50 belongs to the complement.

Exercise 4 · Series and parallel systems

Use \(\Omega=\{WW,WF,FW,FF\}\). The component names in the question overlap with the event names; here we describe events in words.

Series works: {WW}. Series fails: {WF, FW, FF}. Parallel works: {WW, WF, FW}. Parallel fails: {FF}. These match intersection, union, and De Morgan’s rules.

Exercise 6 · Design an eight-outcome experiment

One valid construction: flip three coins and record their order. \(\Omega=\{HHH,HHT,HTH,HTT,THH,THT,TTH,TTT\}\).

Choose A = first toss H, B = first toss T, and C = all tosses H. Then A = {HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT}; B = {THH, THT, TTH, TTT}; C = {HHH}. A and B are disjoint, A ∩ C = {HHH} is nonempty, and Aᶜ = B.

Exercise 6 · Drone checkpoints

Order matters and checkpoints cannot repeat. \(P(8,5)=8\cdot7\cdot6\cdot5\cdot4=6720\). We count ordered visits to five checkpoints; no additional start/end constraints are stated.

Exercise 7 · Robot programs — wording ambiguity

The sentence “5 different actions” literally prohibits repetition, making (a) and (b) identical: \(P(7,5)=2520\) programs each.

If the intended contrast is (a) repetition allowed, (b) no repetition, the answers are \(7^5=16807\) and \(P(7,5)=2520\). State which interpretation you use; the sheet does not resolve this conflict.

Exercise 8 · Words from a 50-letter alphabet

A “word” here means any string, not necessarily a meaningful dictionary word. (a) Repetition allowed: \(50^8=39{,}062{,}500{,}000{,}000\). (b) Twelve distinct letters in order: \(P(50,12)=50!/38!\). The factorial ratio is an exact answer; there is no need to expand a huge integer.

Exercise 9 · A mandatory procedure P

Five distinct procedures are ordered from ten. (a) Choose one of five positions for P, then fill the other four from the nine remaining procedures: \(5P(9,4)=5\cdot3024=15120\).

(b) P first: \(P(9,4)=3024\). (c) P last: also 3024. (d) P somewhere in the middle means positions 2, 3, or 4: \(3P(9,4)=9072\). Check: 3024 + 3024 + 9072 = 15120.

Exercise 10 · Select components for testing

The 12 physical components are distinct, even when they share a type. (a) \(\binom{12}{4}=495\). (b) Select two of five resistors and two of four capacitors: \(\binom52\binom42=60\). No sensors are selected in (b).

Exercise 11 · Engineering teams and a leader

(a) \(\binom{14}{5}=2002\). (b) Exactly three mechanical engineers leaves two electrical engineers: \(\binom83\binom62=840\).

(c) At least two electrical engineers: subtract teams with zero or one from all teams:

\[\binom{14}{5}-\binom85-\binom61\binom84=2002-56-420=1526.\]

For a designated leader, multiply the applicable team count by 5. With no restriction there are \(2002\cdot5=10010\) team + leader assignments. If the final sentence is intended to retain condition (c), there are \(1526\cdot5=7630\); retaining (b) gives 4200.

Exercise 12 · Letters followed by digits

The alphabet has seven letters; digits are 0–9. A zero may occur first in the digit block because this is an ID. Treat (b)–(d) as separate modifications of (a).

(a) \(7^3\cdot10^4=3430000\). (b) Distinct letters: \(P(7,3)10^4=2100000\). (c) Distinct digits, letters may repeat: \(7^3P(10,4)=1728720\).

(d) At least one zero = all digit strings minus strings without zero: \(7^3(10^4-9^4)=1179577\). Choosing a position for a zero and allowing other zeros would count some IDs more than once.
